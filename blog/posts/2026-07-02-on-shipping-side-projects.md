title: "On shipping side projects instead of polishing them forever"
date: "2026-07-02"
tags: ["process"]
excerpt: "Most of my side projects die in the 90% state. Here's the rule I've started using to actually get things out the door."
---

Most side projects don't fail because the idea was bad — they fail because
they never leave the "almost done" state. The last ten percent quietly
expands to fill however much time you're willing to give it.

The rule that's worked for me: ship the thing that's embarrassing, not the
thing that's finished. A live demo with a rough edge beats a polished repo
nobody can try.

Vocation, the academic-pathway planner listed on the projects page above,
went from idea to a deployed hackathon build in about a day. It's not
finished. It's live, and that turned out to matter more.
